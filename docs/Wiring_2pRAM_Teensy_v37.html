2pRAM Teensy 4.1 — wiring changes

Mobile Spouts firmware v37 · shared breakout board · left spout only ·

Summary: 6 new wires, a ground bond, and 5 patch cables

Nothing on the breakout board is modified, cut, or disconnected. No pin is endangered. No ritual is required when swapping firmware between you and your colleague.

This works because the six SMC02 motor lines now live on pins 25–30, which teensy2spout.ino never declares. Under his firmware they stay high-Z, which is "button released", so his sessions cannot move your stages and neither can a Teensy reset. Every other signal you need is a plain TTL output in both firmwares, and only one firmware runs at a time, so there is no contention anywhere.

4. What you add — 6 wires + ground ← the only soldering

4a. SMC02 motor lines

PinToAxis / direction
25X controller — CW button inputX forward
26X controller — CCW button inputX reverse
27Y controller — CW button inputY forward
28Y controller — CCW button inputY reverse
29Z controller — CW button inputZ forward
30Z controller — CCW button inputZ reverse

No STOP wires. In P02 mode the motor halts when CW/CCW is released, and v37 never asserts a STOP line. axisStop() is reachable only from the serial STOP command and releases CW/CCW first regardless, so nothing is lost.

Pins 31–39 remain free if you ever want the per-axis STOP lines back.

4b. Ground bond — required

Button emulation pulls a line down to the SMC02's own control ground. If Teensy GND and SMC02 control GND are not common, "pressed" is undefined and the axis behaves erratically or not at all.

Run a wire from a Teensy GND pin to the control ground of each SMC02. One bond suffices if the three share a supply ground; bonding all three gives lower impedance.

Verify before soldering

  • Pins 25–30 must reach the Teensy directly — a stacking header, ribbon soldered before seating, or pogo pins. This is what makes the whole plan safe; do not substitute pins in 1–24 for convenience. Pin 24 is not safe: it is his DelayIndicatorPin and he does configure it.
  • Is each SMC02 button line ≤3.3 V open-circuit? Teensy 4.1 is not 5 V tolerant and your idle state is a floating pin. If any sits higher, insert an optocoupler or open-drain buffer.
  • Ground loops. You are bonding the Teensy to three stepper-driver grounds while it is also grounded to WaveSurfer through BNC shields. If new noise appears on imaging traces, star-ground the SMC02s instead of tying them to the Teensy, and route motor cable away from signal cable.

5. Never touch

Pins 1, 4, 9, 13, 15, 21, 22, 40, 41. v37 configures none of them in any mode. Pin 4 is the right-spout solenoid and pin 41 its lick detector, both live for your colleague. Pins 9 and 13 have no header. Spare and free: 0, 16, 23, 31–39.

The strobe marks arrival, not trial onset

There is no hardware trial-start signal on this rig. The strobe fires after the move completes — at default calibration that is 2–3 s after the move begins, varying by several hundred ms across positions, because travel distance from dock depends on distance tier and azimuth.

For most analyses this is the marker you want: it sits immediately before settle → pre-cue → cue. But if you align to it as "trial start", you inject a timing offset correlated with spout position — the exact variable you manipulate. True onset is in the session log as EVT name=trial_start; use that if you need the move window.

Note emitPositionCode() also runs on manual moves, tagged manual=1 in the log, so a strobe during setup is not necessarily a trial.

Now identical across all three rigs

2pRAM, GB219 and the Zaber widefield rig all use the same 3-bit position bus plus strobe, the same cue gate, and the same trial-stop pulse. One decoder, one analysis pipeline. The only differences are pin numbers and the motion backend.

7. Bring-up order

  1. Flash v37 (2pRAM build) with motors unconnected. Confirm the GUI connects and GET kind=status responds.
  2. Run LickScan_Teensy.ino. Confirm pin 14 responds to the left spout and idles HIGH, drops LOW on contact. If inverted: SET lick.active_low=false.
  3. Manual reward with one valve connected. Confirm the correct spout clicks — verify against the physical valve, not the SLN silkscreen.
  4. Scope pins 7, 8, 17, 18, 6, 19, 20 and confirm each matches section 6 before trusting any DAQ record. Check that the position bits are stable when the strobe fires.
  5. Power the SMC02s. Connect one axis. Jog ±1 mm from the Motion tab, confirm direction, then re-time ms/mm calibration.
  6. Repeat per axis, then run a full test sequence from the Monitor tab.
  7. Press STOP mid-move. Confirm the stage halts, the remaining axes do not move, and move_aborted appears with a plausible applied_mm.
  8. Re-reference via Motion tab → "Use current XYZ" after any aborted move. No home switches means this is your only ground truth.
  9. Flash your colleague's firmware and confirm the stages stay still through a full session of his. This is the check that validates the whole shared-board approach.
